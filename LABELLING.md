# Labelling guide (version 0.1)

Judge only the text in front of you. The tool hides the URL because the models don't see it either. Long documents are cut at 6,000 characters. Don't look pages up online.

Label quickly and go with your first reasonable judgement. If you can't decide, skip the document as "too unclear": disagreement here is useful evidence that a definition needs work.

## Quality (0 to 5)

Uses [FineWeb-Edu's scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17557), which measures usefulness for teaching at primary and grade-school level, not general writing quality. Add one point for each level met:

| Points | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | No educational content |
| 1 | Some basic relevant information, even with adverts or other noise |
| 2 | Some educational elements, but superficial, mixed with other material, or disorganised |
| 3 | Suitable for teaching and coherent, like a basic tutorial, though it may have limits or be too advanced |
| 4 | Strong teaching material at school level, like a textbook chapter, focused and clear |
| 5 | Outstanding teaching material for school pupils, with detailed reasoning and thorough insight |

A precise legal or technical text can score low here if it wouldn't suit school teaching. That's expected.

## UK relevance

| Answer | Use when | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| UK specific | It's about the UK, or depends on its institutions, places, law or culture | HMRC guidance, a report on a Leeds council meeting |
| UK compatible | It isn't about the UK, but suits a British reader as it stands | An explanation of photosynthesis in British English |
| Non UK | It's about or written for somewhere else, so a British reader would need to adapt it | US tax advice, a local news story from Ohio |

International news reported for a British audience is **UK compatible**. Global facts count as compatible unless the spelling or framing is clearly written for another country.

## Genre

Choose what the text is, not who published it. A press release on a council site is **government** only if it's the council's own official information.

- **news:** reporting or commentary on current events
- **legal:** legislation, case law, contracts, terms and conditions
- **government:** official information or guidance from a public body
- **forum:** discussion threads, comments, Q&A
- **commercial:** selling or promoting a product or service, including company pages
- **academic:** research papers, abstracts, scholarly or course material
- **creative:** fiction, poetry, lyrics, personal essays
- **other:** anything else, including how-to guides, general blogs and reference pages

## Spam or boilerplate

**Yes** if most of the text is SEO filler, keyword or link lists, navigation menus, or repeated template text. An ordinary product page with a real description is **no**: it's commercial, not spam.

## PII risk

**Yes** if the text exposes a private person's home address, personal phone number, private email, or similarly sensitive details. Otherwise **no**, including:

- public figures acting in their public role
- business contact details
- names alone, such as in a news story or a war memorial

If you think a page exposes someone seriously, skip it. Don't quote it anywhere.

## Skipping

Use **upsetting content** freely, with no need to explain. Use **not English** for documents that are mainly in another language.
